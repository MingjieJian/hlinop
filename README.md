# HLINOP

- New version with bug fix thanks to Richard Hoppe, 11 July 2023; see history.txt
- hbop_auto.f added 17 December 2025; small changes to hbop.f
- November 2026: neutral-perturber term switchable (off by default); HLINPROF uses the full Stehle & Hutcheon tables and HBOP uses HLINPROF; HLINOP broadening mechanisms convolved (unit area); test suite; see history.txt

A set of codes for computing hydrogen line profiles and opacities developed by Paul Barklem and Nik Piskunov.  This code was first presented at IAU 210, Modelling Stellar Atmospheres, in Uppsala June 2002 (Barklem & Piskunov 2003). There a need for codes to compute these opacities for atellar atmosphere applications. Codes such as HLINOP and BALMER9 have been provided by Kurucz and Peterson (http://kurucz.harvard.edu/) in the 1970's based on the broadening theories of the time.  Since then, understanding of H line broadening has improved and there is a need to update these codes. Three codes have been produced, namely:

HLINPROF: For detailed, accurate calculation of hydrogen line profiles from the Stark-broadening tables of Stehle & Hutcheon (1999), suitable for detailed analysis of Balmer and other lines. The table `stehle_long.dat` covers Lyman (n_up 2-30), Balmer (3-30) and Paschen (4-30) lines, temperatures 2500 K - 1.26e6 K and electron densities 1e10 - 3.2e19 cm^-3 (fewer densities for higher n_up, up to each line's Inglis-Teller limit). It is built from the CDS catalogue J/A+AS/140/93 with `tools/make_stehle_table.py` (little-endian; see the script for the format). Between the tabulated detunings the profiles are interpolated with a monotone cubic in log S against log alpha (since November 2026; the earlier linear interpolation in these variables made Doppler-dominated cores 1-6 per cent too narrow and lost 1-4 per cent of the area). Above a line's last tabulated density the profile of that density is scaled quasistatically (width proportional to n_e^(2/3)); below 1e10 cm^-3 the first profile is used; other lines and temperatures outside the table go to HLINOP. The tabulated profiles include the thermal Doppler broadening of H; since November 2026 HLINPROF convolves them with the remaining Doppler broadening (the Doppler width passed in beyond the thermal one, e.g. microturbulence) and with the Lorentz profile of the radiative, self and helium widths, which were previously added beyond 0.2 A and so increased the area by up to 4 per cent (`HLINOP_SET_CONVOLUTION(0)` restores the earlier behaviour). Since November 2026 Lyman alpha also comes from the table: the core is the tabulated profile convolved as above (natural, resonance and helium widths), the red side carries the Boltzmann factor exp(-hc dsigma/kT), and beyond -4000 cm^-1 (1278 A) the red wing is the quasi-molecular H-H and H-H+ profiles of Allard et al. (1998) as in HLINOP plus half the tabulated quasistatic wing for the electrons (the H-H+ profiles count protons only), joined to the core linearly in wavelength; with `HLINOP_SET_CONVOLUTION(0)` Lyman alpha goes to HLINOP as before. On the blue side and in the core the earlier HLINOP profile of Lyman alpha is good to n_e ~ 1e14 cm^-3 but has half widths 0.1-0.9 of the tabulated ones and peaks up to 9 times too high at 1e15-1e18 cm^-3. The table file names are compiled in from an include file `DATA.FILES` (see `SYNTH/Makefile` for how to generate it); `CALL HLINPROF_SET_FILES(STEHLE, SELF, VCS)` sets them at run time instead (full paths; a blank name keeps the compiled-in one; call before the first profile). After a call, the integer function `HLINPROF_SOURCE()` tells which profile was returned (1: Stehle & Hutcheon table, 2: VCS table, 0: HLINOP), for codes that report the lines that fall back to HLINOP. The older six-line tables (`stehle.dat.*`) and `hlinprof_nofiles.f` are not used by this version.

HLINOP: For approximate, quick calculation of any line of neutral H, suitable for model atmosphere calculations.  Based on the original Kurucz and Peterson version. Since November 2026 the broadening mechanisms are convolved instead of added: the Stark profile convolves the ion quasistatic profile with the electron profile (impact core and quasistatic electron wing), and the line profile convolves the Doppler profile (with fine structure) with the Lorentz (resonance, van der Waals, natural) and Stark profiles. The earlier code had profile areas of 1.7-2.1; against the Stark-only profiles of Stehle & Hutcheon (1999, Balmer and Paschen) and Lemke (1997, VCS, Brackett) the median half widths and peaks now agree to 2-5 per cent. `CALL HLINOP_SET_CONVOLUTION(0)` restores the earlier profiles; Lyman alpha keeps them (its tabulated profile is in HLINPROF). The convolved profiles (HLINOP and HLINPROF) are computed once per line and plasma state and cached (up to 6144 states per routine), so codes that call the profiles wavelength by wavelength for every depth and line build each state once (about 0.6 ms) and then evaluate it in about 0.1 microsecond. A cached state takes 9.6 kB (about 20 MB for 72 depths and 28 lines; at most 59 MB per routine), and the table with its precomputed interpolation coefficients about 30 MB. `CALL HLINOP_CLEAR_CACHE` empties the caches (results unchanged); a synthesis code calls it when it starts a new model, so that the caches hold one model only (otherwise states of earlier models stay until a cache is full).

HBOP: A wrapper around hlinprof.f and hlinop.f to implement the occupation probability formalism of Daeppen, Anderson and Milhalas (1987) and thus account for the merging of lines into the continuum. The neutral-perturber (excluded-volume) part of the occupation probability is off by default; `CALL HBOP_SET_EXCLUDED_VOLUME(1.0)` before calling HBOP restores the original hard-sphere treatment of Hummer & Mihalas (1988), and values between 0 and 1 scale the excluded volume. Line profiles come from HLINPROF by default (with the HLINOP cutoffs at the series limits), so HBOP needs hlinprof.f and its table; `CALL HBOP_SET_LINE_PROFILE(0)` uses HLINOP for all lines as before.  As bound-free and bound-bound opacities are merged, the distinction between line and continuous opacity becomes blurred, so one should be careful not to include things twice (including Rayleigh scattering off Lyman alpha). For *efficient* implementation you may have to adapt this, in particular so that populations are not recomputed. Codes that compute the line opacities themselves can use the parts on their own: `HBOP_LEVELS` gives the occupation probabilities and LTE populations of the levels (once per depth), and `HBOP_BF` the bound-free opacity including the dissolved levels at a wavelength (equal to the continuum of HBOP).

HBOP_AUTO: This code is a wrapper to HBOP, which will give the total bound-x (bound-bound and bound-free) neutral H opacity at a given wavelength, from the occupation probability formalism, WITHOUT the need for a line list.  This wrapper will simply produce a line list including all lines up to a highest principal quantum number NMAX, in the relevant spectral region (i.e. at the interrogation wavelength).  

The main codes are in the root directory.  An example of their application to the SYNTH spectral synthesis program by Nik Piskunov is also provided.
